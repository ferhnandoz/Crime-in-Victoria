Crime in Victoria: behind the numbers

After falling during the pandemic, recorded crime in Victoria hit a ten-year high in 2025. But "crime" is not one thing. This story looks at what is rising, where it happens, and who is most affected.

01 · The big picture

Down in the pandemic, up to a ten-year high

Victoria Police recorded about 405,000 criminal incidents in the year to June 2017. Numbers dipped during the COVID-19 lockdowns, when people stayed home and shops closed, then climbed to a decade high of 482,134 in 2025. The latest year eased slightly, down 3%.

What is a "criminal incident"? One event reported to or detected by police, which may involve several offences. "Per 100,000 people" divides the count by the population, so places and years with different populations can be compared fairly.

Reading the rankings

Shoplifting (steal from a retail store) dropped to 10th when stores closed in 2021, then shot up to 4th. Incidents rose 78% since 2017.

Motor vehicle theft rose 40%, climbing from 12th to 6th place since 2021.

Home burglary without violence went the other way: down 41%, from 4th to 8th.

Theft from cars has stayed the most common offence every single year.

Nearly half of violent and justice offences involve family violence

Each square is 1% of incidents in the year to June 2026. Red squares are incidents police flagged as family-violence related.

"Justice procedures" includes breaching intervention orders, a common outcome of family violence. Source: Crime Statistics Agency, state Table 03.

03 · Where it happens

Regional cities have some of the highest rates

Victoria has 79 local councils. Apart from inner Melbourne, many of the highest crime rates are in regional centres such as Latrobe (Morwell, Traralgon), Mildura and Greater Shepparton. Click any council to see how its rate has changed over ten years.

04 · Crime and disadvantage

Crime is higher where people have less

The Australian Bureau of Statistics scores every council on socio-economic disadvantage, using things like income, unemployment and education (the SEIFA index). Lower scores mean more disadvantage. Combining this with crime data shows a clear pattern.

Two maps in one: crime rate and disadvantage

Councils are split into thirds on each measure. Dark purple = high crime and high disadvantage; red = high crime, low disadvantage; blue = high disadvantage, low crime.

15 councils are in the highest third for both. Numbers in the key show how many councils fall in each group. Sources: Crime Statistics Agency (2026); ABS SEIFA 2021, Index of Relative Socio-economic Disadvantage.

In the most disadvantaged councils, the typical crime rate (7,494) is 63% higher than in the least disadvantaged (4,599).

05 · Solved or not?

More crimes are going unsolved

Police record the outcome of each incident. "Unsolved" means no offender has been identified. For all five main types of crime, the unsolved share is higher than in 2017. One in four crimes against the person now goes unsolved, up from one in seven.

2017 vs 2026, by type of crime.

Source: Crime Statistics Agency, state Table 04 (charge status).

What this means

Most property crime, such as theft from cars, has always been hard to solve: three in four incidents stay unsolved.

The bigger change is in violent and public order offences, where the unsolved share has roughly doubled or more.

So Victoria's crime picture is less about "more crime" overall and more about which crimes, where, and how many lead to a result.
